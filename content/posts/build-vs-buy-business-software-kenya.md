Before spending on software, ask a simple question: is there already a tool that does what I need?

## When ready-made software is enough

- Your process is standard, such as basic accounting or email marketing
- You need to start immediately
- Your budget is small
- The tool is widely used and well supported

## When custom software makes sense

- Your process is unusual and gives you an advantage
- You pay for several tools that do not work together
- You need features that ready-made products lack
- You have outgrown a tool's limits
- You want to own and control your system

## Comparing the two

| | Ready-made | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Starting cost | Lower | Higher |
| Long-term cost | Subscriptions continue | Mostly one-time build plus maintenance |
| Fit to your process | You adapt to it | It adapts to you |
| Speed to start | Fast | Takes time to build |
| Flexibility | Limited | High |

## A middle path

Many businesses start with ready-made tools, then build custom pieces or integrations where the gaps hurt most.
