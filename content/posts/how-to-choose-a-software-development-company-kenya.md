Picking the wrong developer can cost you money, time and sometimes your business data. These eight questions help you choose well.

## 1. Can I see work you have done?
Ask for real projects and, if possible, speak to a past client. Look at how the finished product works, not only how it looks.

## 2. Who will actually build my software?
Find out whether it is an in-house team or a middleman who outsources the work.

## 3. How will we communicate?
Agree on update schedules and who your contact person is.

## 4. What is the full cost?
Get a written scope and price. Ask what is not included, such as hosting, domain and changes after launch.

## 5. Who owns the code and the accounts?
You should own your software, domain and hosting accounts.

## 6. How do you handle changes?
Every project changes along the way. Ask how extra requests are priced.

## 7. What happens after launch?
Find out about support, bug fixes and updates, and what they cost.

## 8. Do you understand my business?
A good developer asks about your customers and processes before talking about technology.

## Red flags

- Very low quotes with vague scope
- No portfolio or references
- Pressure to pay everything upfront
- Developer keeps ownership of your accounts
