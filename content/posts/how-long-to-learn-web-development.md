Everyone wants a number. The honest answer is that it depends on how much time you put in and how well you are guided. But there are useful benchmarks.

## A realistic timeline

| Stage | What you learn | Typical time |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | HTML and CSS | 3 to 6 weeks |
| Programming | JavaScript | 6 to 10 weeks |
| Frameworks | React and Next.js | 6 to 10 weeks |
| Backend and data | APIs and databases | 4 to 8 weeks |
| Portfolio | Real projects | Ongoing |

For someone studying several hours a day, that is around four to six months to become job-ready. Studying part-time takes longer.

## What speeds you up

- A clear structure instead of random tutorials
- Building projects from the first week
- Getting feedback on your code
- Studying a little every day

## What slows you down

- Switching courses and languages
- Watching without practising
- Learning alone with no one to ask
