If you run a business in Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu or anywhere else in Kenya, you have probably asked this question: **how much should a website actually cost?** Search around and you will see quotes from KES 3,000 to KES 500,000. That range is so wide it is almost useless.

This guide breaks down what you really pay for, what the typical price ranges are in 2026, and how to pick the option that fits your business without overpaying.

## The short answer

For a professional business website with around 5 to 7 pages, a contact form and a mobile-friendly design, most Kenyan businesses should budget **KES 25,000 to KES 50,000** for the build. Add roughly **KES 2,000 to KES 5,000 per year** for your domain and hosting. Online shops, booking systems and custom web apps cost more, and we cover those below.

## What you are actually paying for

A website is not one purchase. It is a few separate costs, and knowing them helps you compare quotes fairly.

### 1. Domain name (about KES 600 to KES 3,000 per year)

This is your address on the internet, like yourbusiness.co.ke or yourbusiness.com. A .co.ke domain is usually the cheapest, while .com domains cost a bit more. You renew it every year.

### 2. Web hosting (about KES 1,500 to KES 5,000 per year for most small business sites)

Hosting is the space where your website lives so people can visit it any time. Shared hosting is enough for most small business sites. Bigger shops or high-traffic sites may need a VPS, which costs more each month.

### 3. Design and development (the biggest cost)

This is the work of planning, designing, building and testing your site. It is where prices vary the most, because it depends on who builds it and how much work is involved.

### 4. Content: text, photos and logo

A site with clear writing and real photos of your business converts far better than one filled with generic stock images. If you do not have these ready, budget for them or ask your developer to help.

### 5. Ongoing maintenance

Security updates, backups, fixing things that break and making small changes all take time. Some developers include a few months of support, others charge a monthly fee.

## Typical price ranges in Kenya

| Type of website | Typical cost (KES) | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| DIY website (you build it yourself) | 2,000 to 12,000 per year | Very small businesses with time and patience |
| Simple business website (5 to 7 pages) | 25,000 to 50,000 | Service businesses, clinics, schools, law firms, salons |
| Business website with a CMS or blog you can edit yourself | 35,000 to 70,000 | Businesses that publish content often |
| Booking or scheduling system | 40,000 to 80,000 | Hotels, clinics, salons, tour operators |
| Online store with M-Pesa | 80,000 to 250,000+ | Shops selling products online |
| Custom web application | 100,000 to 300,000+ | Businesses with unique processes (POS, portals, dashboards) |

These are market ballparks, not fixed prices. Your final cost depends on your exact needs.

## What makes a website cost more or less

**Number of pages.** A 5-page site takes far less work than a 30-page one.

**Custom design vs. templates.** A template-based design is faster and cheaper. A fully custom design built around your brand costs more.

**Features.** A simple contact form is cheap. M-Pesa payments, user accounts, booking calendars, search and admin dashboards all add development time.

**Content readiness.** If you hand over finished text and photos, the project moves faster. If your developer has to write copy and source images, expect a higher price.

**Who builds it.** A student or beginner freelancer may charge very little but carry more risk. An experienced developer or agency costs more but usually delivers faster, with fewer problems and proper support.

**Timeline.** Rush jobs often cost extra.

## The hidden costs to ask about

A cheap quote can turn expensive if these are not discussed upfront:

- **Who owns the domain and hosting?** Make sure they are registered in your name, not the developer's.
- **Are revisions included?** Ask how many rounds of changes are covered.
- **Is SSL (the padlock) included?** Every site should have it. Many hosts offer it free.
- **What happens after launch?** Ask about support, backups and security.
- **Can you edit the site yourself?** If you cannot update your own prices and photos, every small change costs you money.

## Is a cheap website worth it?

Sometimes. If you are just starting out and need an online presence quickly, a simple low-cost site is a fair first step. But be careful with quotes far below the market, such as KES 5,000 or KES 10,000 for a full business site. Common problems include:

- Generic templates that look like everyone else's
- Slow loading and poor mobile display
- No security or backups
- A developer who disappears after payment

Your website is often the first thing a potential customer sees. A weak one can cost you more in lost clients than you saved.

## How to think about the return

Instead of asking "what does it cost?", ask "what will it earn?" If a KES 40,000 website brings you just a handful of new customers a month, each spending a few thousand shillings, it can pay for itself within a few months. Service businesses such as lawyers, contractors, clinics and trainers often win a single client who covers the whole cost.

## How to get an accurate quote

Before you contact any developer, prepare answers to these questions:

1. What is the main goal of the website: get calls, sell products, take bookings or share information?
2. How many pages do you think you need?
3. Do you need online payments (M-Pesa, card)?
4. Do you have a logo, brand colours, text and photos?
5. Do you want to update the site yourself?
6. What is your budget range and when do you need it live?

With these answers, any developer can give you a realistic quote within a day.
